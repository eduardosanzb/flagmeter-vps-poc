BCG Slide Principles

best practices

Rules from McKinsey, BCG, and Minto's Pyramid Principle. Follow these and your slides will communicate faster and land harder.

40 The Word Limit

Every slide has 40 words or fewer. If you need more — you have two slides. Count before you ship.

Meanwhile, one engineer holds the mental model for all of it. Your team ships features around infra constraints. Hotfixes take 38 minutes instead of 2...

→ One engineer holds the model. They leave — MTTR = unknown.

→ Action Titles

The title states the conclusion, not the topic. The audience should understand your point from the title alone — even without the body.

Cost Analysis

Infrastructure Overview

THE BILL DOESN'T SPIKE. IT CREEPS.

¶ No Paragraphs

Paragraphs force reading. Statements allow scanning. Use statements: arrays — each point must survive alone.

statements:

- "Point one. Short."

- "Point two. Alone."

- "Point three. Done."

2/3 White Space Rule

Two-thirds of every slide should be empty. Negative space is not wasted space — it's where the message lands. If it looks sparse, it's probably right.

3 Points Maximum

If you have four things to say, you have two slides. Three is the maximum the brain holds without a numbered list. Fewer is always better.

5-5-5: ≤5 words/bullet · ≤5 bullets · ≤5 min/section

△ Pyramid Principle

Conclusion first. Evidence second. Context last. The reader should never have to wait for your point — state it in the title, prove it in the body.

1. State the answer.

2. Show the proof.

3. Provide the context.

How to Write Slides

Create a .en.md file inside content/slides/. Define your slides as a YAML array in the frontmatter. Each slide needs a type that determines its visual layout.

Frontmatter Schema

---
title: "My Deck Title"
description: "Short description shown on the index"
slides:
  - number: 1
    type: hero
    label: "Section Label"
    title: "BIG SLIDE TITLE"
    subtitle: "Supporting line under the title"
    # ... type-specific fields (see below)
---
